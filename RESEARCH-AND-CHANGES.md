# CloseAgain refinement

The supplied archive is preserved as the initial local Git commit. This working copy is independent of other CloseAgain projects.

## Design decisions

- Keep the cream paper, editorial serif headline, vermilion ribbon, and brand statement.
- Let the ribbon explain progress. The new/older lead paths share one scroll timeline; their branches meet after the steps rather than independently drawing on unrelated timers. Supporting-page rails follow reading progress. Scrolling remains native.
- A concise interactive conversation shows a new inquiry or quiet lead moving toward a team handoff. Examples remain labeled illustrative.
- Replace repeated homepage feature explanations with concrete control: approved wording, schedules, stop on reply, and human handoff.
- Preserve the four plan prices and existing capability boundaries. Make differences scannable with three aligned tiers, an Enterprise band, and a use-case selector. No invented popularity, discounts, allowances, trial, or conversion figures.
- Show the confirmed business email, with a direct email contact route when server form delivery is unconfigured.

## Research used

- [NN/g: Comparison tables](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/comparison-tables/) — align meaningful differences and keep the comparison easy to scan.
- [NN/g: Pricing information on B2B sites](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/) — retain visible pricing during buyer research.
- [web.dev: High-performance CSS animations](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide) — prefer transforms and opacity over layout-changing animation; avoid gratuitous permanent layer hints.
- [GSAP: ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/) — coordinate section progress without replacing native scrolling.
- [GSAP: matchMedia](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia()/) — scope animations and clean them up across routes and reduced-motion changes.
- [W3C: Tabs pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/tabs/) and [accessible tables](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/tables/) — keyboard-operable choices and semantic comparisons.

These are design and implementation decisions, not claims of measured conversion improvement. The delivered changes should be evaluated with real visitor behavior once launched.
